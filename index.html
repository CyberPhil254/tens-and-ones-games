For grown-ups

  • Play today's games is the main button: a short warm-up from games she already knows, then two rounds of the next game. It takes about ten minutes. Then stop. Short and daily works better than long and weekly.
  • The game moves her up a level when she wins five or six bones in a round, and down again when a round is too hard. A perfect round skips a level. A game is finished after two good rounds at its last level, and it comes back if she misses it twice in a later warm-up. You can still pick any game and any level yourself.
  • A bone means right first time. A paw means right after a hint or with the help picture.
  • The questions and hints are read aloud by a recorded voice. Tap the speaker next to a question to hear it again.
  • Sit with her when you can and ask “How did you know?”. Saying it in tens and ones is where it sticks.
  • Race to 100 is for two players. Say the numbers aloud and count on from where you stand, never from 1.
  • Do the same with real things: bundles of ten straws and loose straws, or 10 and 1 shilling coins.
  • The last level of the adding and taking away games hides the blocks on purpose. Quick Paws opens only when Friends of Ten and Jump in Tens are finished: speed comes after getting it right.
  • Tens stand in groups of five, like the ones. Eight tens should read as “five and three”. Fingers are allowed, they fade by themselves.
  • Jump in Tens practises test question 12, Fill the Ten question 18, Break a Ten question 14 and Mystery Box question 20.
