Tens & Ones

Every number is made of tens and ones. Press the buttons and watch what happens.

Eight games

Win a bone for the puppy every time you are right first go.

    For grown-ups

    • Play the games in order, 1 to 8. Games 1 to 3 are the foundation: stay there until she stops counting one by one. Game 5 practises test question 12, game 6 question 18, game 7 question 14 and game 8 question 20.
    • Five to ten minutes a day, one or two games, then stop. Short and daily works better than long and weekly.
    • Ask “How do you know?” and let her explain it with tens and ones. Saying it aloud is where it sticks.
    • Do the same with real things: bundles of ten straws and loose straws, or 10 and 1 shilling coins.
    • A bone means right first time, a paw means right after a hint. Go up a level when she gets five or six bones without counting one by one. Fingers are allowed, they fade by themselves.
    • Tens stand in groups of five, just like the ones. Eight tens should read as “five and three”, not be counted one by one.
    • There is no timer on purpose. A wrong answer gives a hint and another try.
