# What each game practises

Written by Claude Code from the code of the live game (`index.html`), for the planning chat to build the skill map from. It describes what the games do today, in plain words. It contains no curriculum wording and no curriculum codes.

The number ranges are not estimates: they come from the reference check in `app/src/reference/`, which draws every game and level until no new question turns up. "Questions" is the count of different questions a level can ask.

How to read the answer column: **four choices** means four number buttons in rising order; **pad** means the buttons 1 to 9; **build** and **tap** mean the child makes the answer herself, so there is nothing to pick from.

## The eleven games on the path

### 1. High Five

Seeing a small amount as "five and some more" without counting one by one.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sees fingers and a tower of ones (first five dark, rest light) side by side, both stay on screen. Says how many | 1 to 10 | Four choices | 10 |
| 2 | Sees the tower for about two seconds, then it is covered. She may peek again. The fingers appear after she answers | 3 to 10 | Four choices | 8 |
| 3 | Same, with a ten frame instead of the tower | 3 to 10 | Four choices | 8 |

### 2. Pack Ten

Ten ones make one ten; a two-digit number is so many tens and so many ones.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A heap of loose ones. She packs them into tens, ten at a time, until fewer than ten are left, then says how many there are | 11 to 39, never a round ten | Four choices | 27 |
| 2 | Same with more | 31 to 69, never a round ten | Four choices | 36 |
| 3 | The other way round: she is given a number and builds it from tens and ones. Ten ones she adds pack themselves into a ten | 11 to 99, no zero in either digit | Build | 81 |

### 3. Quick Look

Reading a two-digit number from its tens and ones blocks, at a glance.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sees the blocks with the count written under the tens (10, 20, 30). Says how many | 11 to 59, round tens included | Four choices | 49 |
| 2 | Same without the counting labels (they appear after a wrong answer) | 11 to 99 | Four choices | 89 |
| 3 | The blocks show for about two seconds, then are covered. She may peek again | 11 to 99 | Four choices | 89 |

Wrong choices are one ten or one one away, and the number with its digits swapped (47 and 74).

### 4. Number Names

Matching number names to numerals, and knowing which digit says the tens.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reads a number written as a word and picks the numeral | 13 to 99. About a third are the teens 13 to 19, a quarter are round tens 30 to 90 | Four choices | 86 |
| 2 | "Which number has 6 tens?" or "Which number has 6 ones?" (tens about two times in three) | Choices are two-digit numbers, 10 to 98; one choice always has the same digit in the other place | Four choices | 153 |
| 3 | Hears the number, nothing written, and picks the numeral | As level 1 | Four choices | 86 |

Wrong choices are chosen to catch the usual mix-ups: sixteen and sixty, 17 and 71.

### 5. Puppy Walk

Where a number sits on a line from 0 to 100, with its tens and ones laid end to end along the line.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The puppy stands on the line with the tens and ones laid out up to it. She says which number | 11 to 99, no zero in either digit | Four choices | 81 |
| 2 | She walks the puppy to a given number with "Jump 10" and "Step 1" | Same | Build | 81 |
| 3 | A line with only a few numbers marked. She taps where the number belongs; within 5 either side counts as right | 6 to 94 | Tap | 89 |

### 6. Friends of Ten

Pairs that make 10, then the gap from a two-digit number to the next ten.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sees fingers and a ten frame. "How many more to make 10?" | 1 to 9 | Pad | 9 |
| 2 | Same as a number sentence, 7 + ? = 10. The picture is hidden until she answers | 1 to 9 | Pad | 9 |
| 3 | 47 + ? = 50, with blocks | Start 11 to 89, never a round ten | Pad | 72 |

### 7. Jump in Tens

Adding and taking away tens: only the tens digit changes.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | On a hundred square, taps where 10 more or 10 fewer lands | Start 1 to 99, never a round ten; result 1 to 99 | Tap | 162 |
| 2 | Same with 20 or 30 | Same | Tap | 270 |
| 3 | Counts on or back in tens: taps the next three numbers | Same | Tap | 126 |
| 4 | In her head: 47 + 30 as a number sentence, jumps of 10 to 40. The hundred square is there if she asks, and then the answer no longer earns a bone | Same | Four choices | 540 |

### 8. Fill the Ten

Adding a one-digit number to a two-digit number by filling up to the next ten first.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The second number fills the ten exactly (47 + 3) | First number 11 to 89, never a round ten; sum 20 to 90 | Four choices | 72 |
| 2 | The sum goes past the ten (47 + 5), in two guided steps: how many fill the ten, then the ten and the rest | First number 15 to 89 with 5 to 9 ones; second number 2 to 9; sum 21 to 98 | Pad, then four choices | 240 |
| 3 | The same sums in one step | Same | Four choices | 240 |
| 4 | In her head: a mix of level 1 sums (about 4 in 10) and level 3 sums. A picture is there if she asks, and costs the bone | Sum 20 to 98 | Four choices | 312 |

### 9. Break a Ten

Taking away from a two-digit number, tens first, breaking a ten into ones when the ones run out.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Takes away whole tens (57 − 20) from the blocks, then says what is left | From 20 to 99; take away 10 to 80; left 10 to 89 | Four choices | 360 |
| 2 | Takes away tens and ones with enough ones to do it (57 − 23) | From 22 to 99; take away 11 to 89; left 10 to 88 | Four choices | 1584 |
| 3 | Not enough ones (52 − 27): she must break a ten into ten ones first | From 30 to 98; take away 11 to 79; left 11 to 79 | Four choices | 1260 |
| 4 | In her head: half level 2 sums, half level 3 sums. The blocks are there if she asks, and cost the bone | As levels 2 and 3 | Four choices | 2844 |

The answer is never less than 10.

### 10. Mystery Box

Finding a missing number, shown as a whole bar and its parts.

| Level | What the child does | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One part missing: ? + 4 = 10 or 4 + ? = 10 (about 7 in 10), or 13 + ? = 20 | Whole 10 or 20; answer 1 to 9 | Pad | 36 |
| 2 | Three parts, the middle one missing: 3 + ? + 5 = 14 | Parts 1 to 7; whole 10 to 20; answer 1 to 9 | Pad | 347 |
| 3 | With tens, in three forms: ? − 20 = 34, ? + 20 = 54, 54 − ? = 34 | Tens 10 to 40; the other number 11 to 59, never a round ten; whole 21 to 99 | Four choices | 540 |

### 11. Puppy Stories

Short word problems. Every story is followed by its number sentence; the blocks appear if she asks or after a wrong answer.

| Level | Stories | Numbers | Answer | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has some, finds 10 more. Has some, eats 10. Wants 10, has some: how many more? | 11 to 99 for the first two; 1 to 9 for the third | Four choices | 167 |
| 2 | Finds 20, 30 or 40 more. Gives 20, 30 or 40 away. Digs up exactly enough to fill the ten (47 and 3) | 11 to 99 | Four choices | 426 |
| 3 | Finds a few more, past the ten (47 and 5). Wants more than he has, the gap being 10 to 40. Hides some, tens and ones, no ten to break (57 hides 23) | 11 to 99 | Four choices | 2080 |

The three kinds of story at each level come up equally often.

## Outside the path

- **Warm-up** (start of the daily session): five questions mixed from the games already finished, each at the level the child last reached. Pack Ten is left out of the mix.
- **Quick Paws**: ten questions for speed, drawn from Friends of Ten level 2, Jump in Tens level 4 and Number Names level 1. Unlocks when Friends of Ten and Jump in Tens are finished. The time is shown at the end against her own best, not while she plays.
- **Race to 100**: a board game for two on the hundred square. A spin gives 1, 2, 3 or 10 (10 twice as often). The player taps the square she lands on, counting on from where she stands.
- **Number machine**: free play. A number from 0 to 99 as blocks, with buttons for one ten or one one more or fewer.

## What no game asks today

Plain facts about the present games, for whoever looks for gaps:

- Nothing goes above 100, and 100 itself appears only as the end of the race and the number line.
- No question has zero as its answer, and take-away answers are never below 10.
- Adding is a two-digit number plus one digit, or plus whole tens. Two two-digit numbers are never added (take-away does have two two-digit numbers).
- No adding or taking away within 20 that crosses ten (8 + 5, 13 − 6), except as part of a larger sum.
- No comparing or ordering of numbers, no odd and even, no counting in steps other than tens.
- No doubling, halving, sharing, groups, money, fractions or measures.
